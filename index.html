HVAC Knowledge & Guidance

Why Proactive HVAC Care Matters

Your cooling and heating system is one of the most hardworking mechanical systems in your building. Understanding efficiency, warning signs, and proper maintenance keeps costs low and comfort high.

Lower Energy Costs

A neglected air conditioner loses approximately 5% of its overall operating efficiency for every year it operates without a tune-up. Dirty condenser coils, low refrigerant levels, or clogged blower wheels force the compressor to run longer cycles, directly driving up monthly electricity expenses.

Signs Your System Needs Service

Watch for telltale warning signs: weak airflow from registers, musty odors upon start-up, unexplained squealing or grinding noises, frequent short-cycling, or persistent ice buildup along copper suction lines. Addressing these early prevents catastrophic motor burnout.

Extended System Lifespan

Standard central A/C units and heat pumps are engineered to run reliably for 12 to 15 years when properly serviced. Consistent professional maintenance protects essential components—like the reversing valve, capacitor, and scroll compressor—from premature thermal breakdown.

We recommend having your cooling system professionally inspected and serviced at least once a year, ideally in early spring before high-demand summer usage. For combined heat pump or HVAC systems that run year-round, bi-annual maintenance (spring and autumn) is strongly advised.

Warm air typically points to one of several issues: a severely clogged air filter restricting air volume, low refrigerant due to a leak, a tripped outside condenser breaker, or a faulty run capacitor preventing the compressor from engaging. A general rule of thumb is the $5,000 rule: multiply the age of the system (in years) by the estimated repair cost. If the result exceeds $5,000, replacement is often more cost-effective. Also consider replacement if your equipment is over 12-15 years old, uses obsolete refrigerant, or requires frequent costly repairs.

Standard 1-inch fiberglass filters should typically be inspected every 30 days and replaced every 1 to 3 months. Homes with pets, residents with allergies, or high dust levels should replace them every 30 to 45 days to maintain clean airflow and prevent frozen coils.
