Warmth & Safety

Efficient Heating When Cooler Weather Arrives

When temperatures drop, having a heating system that engages immediately and safely is essential. In warm coastal regions, the vast majority of residential homes rely on reverse-cycle heat pumps or supplemental electric heat strip assemblies integrated directly into their indoor air handlers.

At A1 A/C Service, we specialize in heat pump optimization. If your heat pump is blowing cold air in heating mode, stuck in a defrost cycle, or running up high electricity bills because it is stuck on emergency auxiliary heat, our technicians will diagnose the reversing valve, defrost control sensor, and electric element contactors to restore safe, comfortable heating.

Troubleshooting

Common Heating & Heat Pump Symptoms

Do not wait until a freeze to discover your heating unit is out of service.

Blowing Cool Air in Heat Mode

Indicates a failed reversing valve solenoid or low refrigerant preventing the heat pump from absorbing ambient outdoor heat.

Outdoor Unit Frozen in Ice

The defrost control board or defrost thermostat sensor has failed, allowing frost to blanket the outdoor heat pump coils.

Auxiliary "AUX" Heat Stays On

Electric heat strips running constantly consume triple the electricity of heat pump operation, causing power bill shock.

Burnt Electrical Smell

Dust burning off elements during first seasonal use is normal for 10 minutes; persistent burning odors indicate failing wiring.

Our Process

Heating System Inspection Sequence

1

Thermostat Staging

We test heating calls from your thermostat to verify primary heat pump engagement and auxiliary heat sequencing.

2

Reversing Valve Test

We verify that the 4-way valve shifts smoothly between cooling and heating modes with zero internal bypass leakage.

3

Element & Amp Check

We measure current draw across each heating bank to ensure all coils heat evenly and limits disengage safely.

4

Defrost Simulation

We force a defrost cycle to verify that outdoor ice will melt rapidly during near-freezing ambient temperatures.

5

Air Temperature Rise

We measure heat rise across the supply plenum to verify manufacturer-specified thermal performance.

Heating Frequently Asked Questions

Unlike gas furnaces that blow 120°F air, heat pumps deliver steady 90°F to 95°F air, which warms your home effectively but can feel slightly cool against human skin (98.6°F). However, if the air feels distinctly cold (under 80°F), your reversing valve or refrigerant circuit requires repair.

Light frost is normal, but the unit should automatically enter defrost mode every 30 to 90 minutes to melt it away. If ice encases the entire unit, the defrost control board or sensor has failed and must be serviced immediately.
